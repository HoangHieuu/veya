Round 1 - Idea Proposal
1. Product/project name
- Product name: Veya
Veya is an AI-powered travel discovery and route-personalisation layer for Vietnam Airlines’ Australian website. It
helps individual travellers turn a vague trip idea into a curated, Vietnam Airlines-compatible route
recommendation, then hands them into the existing official flight-search flow with their preferences pre-filled.

3. Chosen Problem Statement and Reason for the Choice
- Problem Setter: Vietnam Airlines
- Official problem statement: How might we make vietnamairlines.com and our mobile app the smartest,
most personalised and most rewarding place to book — so travellers choose us directly instead of through
third-party online travel agents (OTAs)?
- Our focus within this challenge: Vietnam Airlines has clarified that OTAs win largely because they let
undecided travellers compare multiple airlines and destinations in one place, while vietnamairlines.com
currently offers a single-airline, From/To/Date search form. We focus on that earlier, pre-booking discovery
moment: turning a vague trip idea into a Vietnam Airlines-compatible route recommendation, then
handing the traveller into Vietnam Airlines’ existing search and booking flow with their choice already
pre-filled. We do not redesign booking, payment or checkout, which are operated by third-party systems
outside Vietnam Airlines’ direct control, and we target individual leisure/VFR/student travellers in
Australia rather than corporate travellers, who are served through a separate channel.
- Why we chose this problem:
- Root-cause fit: Vietnam Airlines has confirmed travellers often turn to OTAs before they have
chosen a destination or airline, a discovery problem that natural-language AI and explainable
recommendation are well suited to solve, rather than one that needs backend booking or pricing
access we do not have.
- Explicitly in-scope: the clarification notes name search experience, content, promotion,
engagement and loyalty as areas Vietnam Airlines can change; Veya operates entirely within that
scope and does not touch booking, payment or checkout.
- Team fit: our members combine backgrounds in machine learning, product/UX design, and
business analytics, suited to a problem that is as much about discovery and trust as it is about AI.
- Depth over breadth: we scope to a small, curated set of Australian departure cities and Vietnam
Airlines-served destinations so the team can build an explainable, working demo rather than
attempting broad, shallow coverage.
4. Problem Framing
- The specific pain point: On vietnamairlines.com and the Vietnam Airlines app, a traveller without a fixed
destination or airline in mind is met with a bare From/To/Date search form. This works well for someone
who already knows what they want, but offers no help to someone still deciding where, when or how to
travel, so they turn to an OTA, which lets them compare airlines and destinations side by side. Vietnam
Airlines has confirmed that many visitors check flight schedules on its site but do not complete a direct
booking, which points to a discovery gap rather than only a pricing gap.
- Primary, secondary and pilot users:
- Primary users: individual leisure, VFR (visiting friends and relatives) and student travellers based
in Australia who have a trip in mind but have not yet chosen a destination, route or airline.
Corporate travellers are out of scope, as they are served through a separate booking channel.
- Secondary users: Vietnam Airlines’ Australia-market commercial and e-commerce team, who
want more visitors to complete a direct search and booking rather than leaving for an OTA to find
inspiration.
- Pilot persona: Olivia, based in Sydney, has 8–10 days free in November and wants a
beach-and-food trip in Asia with a friend, low-hassle and few connections, but has not yet decided
on a country or airline.
- Pilot scenario:
- Pilot coverage: Australian departure cities (Sydney, Melbourne, Perth) matched to a curated set
of Vietnam Airlines-served destinations (e.g. Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi, Da Nang).
- Pilot experience: one guided session from a natural-language trip brief to 3 ranked
destination/route cards to a pre-filled hand-off into Vietnam Airlines’ existing search flow.
- Market context and research plan:
- Market context: Vietnam Airlines has confirmed that OTAs’ core advantage is letting travellers
compare multiple airlines and destinations in one place, while a single airline’s own website can
only show that airline’s own fares and routes - a structural discovery gap rather than a pricing gap,
most acute for visitors who check schedules on vietnamairlines.com without completing a
booking.
- Observed user gap: A traveller who has not yet chosen a destination or airline has little reason to
start on a single-airline site; they need help deciding where and how to travel before they can
meaningfully compare fares.
- Research plan: The team will review Vietnam Airlines’ confirmed Australia-origin route network
and any available destination or promotional content to build a curated dataset, and will test the
prototype with 5–8 Australia-based travellers to check whether the recommended routes and
“why this fits” explanations make sense and would make them consider starting their search on
vietnamairlines.com.

5. Assumptions & Open Questions
- Assumptions:
- Vietnam Airlines has confirmed it does not yet have a deep, verified individual preference database.
Veya therefore relies only on self-declared, session-based preferences (a short quiz or
natural-language trip brief), not historical personalisation; deeper personalisation is treated as a
future possibility, not a Round 2 dependency.
- We assume the MVP can use a curated, static dataset of confirmed and illustrative
Australia-to-Vietnam routes, destination content and promotions, with any unconfirmed route or
schedule information explicitly labelled illustrative.
- We assume the solution sits entirely before the existing booking flow and only pre-fills the existing
Vietnam Airlines search (origin, destination, dates); we do not redesign search-results ranking,
payment or checkout, which remain outside Vietnam Airlines’ direct control.
- We assume the primary platform is Vietnam Airlines’ main website and app; Lotusmiles may be
referenced as an optional loyalty/engagement signal but is not redesigned.
- We assume trip-archetype background images (beach, education, food & culture, family, etc.) can
be sourced from licensed stock libraries or imagery approved by Vietnam Airlines or its
tourism-board partners; no scraped or unlicensed imagery will be used, even in the prototype.
- Open Questions:
- Which Australia-origin routes and connections (from Sydney, Melbourne, Perth) can Vietnam
Airlines confirm for the destinations we plan to feature, so our route cards rely on confirmed data
rather than illustrative placeholders?
- What destination, seasonal or promotional content can Vietnam Airlines provide or approve for
our curated knowledge base, versus content we should treat as illustrative only?
- Should the prototype assume any session-level signal beyond a self-declared quiz (e.g. a logged-in
Lotusmiles tier), or should personalisation rely solely on what the traveller tells us in the moment?
- Is there a preferred way to demonstrate the hand-off into the existing search flow (e.g. a mock deep
link with pre-filled parameters), given we should not touch the real booking or payment system?
6. Proposed Solution
- Veya has four components that sit entirely within the search, content, personalisation and engagement
layer Vietnam Airlines can control, before a traveller reaches the existing booking and payment flow, which
we do not modify:
- AI Trip Brief: The traveller types a natural-language trip idea or answers a short quiz (travel style,
date flexibility, budget band, number of travellers, priority). A large language model extracts this
into a structured, validated intent record.
- Veya engine: A small set of curated, Vietnam Airlines-compatible destinations and routes is
ranked against the traveller's intent using a transparent, weighted-scoring approach: each candidate
route is scored on how well it matches the traveller's stated intent, how well the dates fit, how
convenient the route is (direct vs. connecting), how well it fits the traveller's budget band, how
much loyalty value it carries, and whether a relevant promotion applies, then combined into one
overall ranking. The traveller can choose a priority preset ("Lowest hassle", "Best for family",
"Maximise miles", "Food & culture") that adjusts how much each factor counts, automatically; the
underlying weighting stays hidden from the traveller-facing screen and is only auditable in a
separate judge/dev view, keeping the recommendation explainable without exposing controls the
traveller doesn't need. If a field is missing for a given destination (for example, no promotion or
Lotusmiles value on file), it is simply scored as zero rather than causing an error. Because the MVP
does not integrate real-time fare data, the budget-fit factor is based on an illustrative, self-declared
band (e.g. traveller-selected budget / standard / premium) rather than a live price signal, clearly
noted as such. Each destination/route record includes departure city, destination, indicated
connections (direct, or connecting and via which hub), typical flight duration, seasonality notes, a
short "getting around" summary (typical airport-to-city transport options and approximate
duration), a trip-archetype tag (e.g. beach/relaxation, education, food & culture, family) with an
associated licensed background image, source document and owner, and a flag for whether the
route or schedule data is confirmed or illustrative. Veya only recommends a destination or route
present in the curated dataset. Any illustrative route is labelled clearly, for example: "Illustrative
route availability for prototype purposes; final recommendations require Vietnam
Airlines-approved schedule and inventory data." For instance, Da Nang is labelled a one-stop
connection via Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City rather than a direct flight, consistent with Vietnam
Airlines' current network, so the route-convenience factor never overstates its convenience.
- Destination story cards, promotion and rewards preview: For each recommended route, a
context-grounded card explains "why this fits", shows a suggested Vietnam Airlines route from the
traveller's city, an illustrative trip outline, a short "getting around" note (for example, typical taxi or
airport-bus time from the destination airport into the city centre), and, only when present in the
curated dataset, a relevant promotion or an indicative Lotusmiles value. Each card also carries a
background image matched to the traveller's trip archetype, for example a beach scene for a leisure
trip, a university campus for an education-focused trip, or a street-food scene for a
food-and-culture trip, using only licensed or tourism-board-approved imagery, to make the
destination feel tangible and encourage the traveller to keep going. Nothing shown on a card is left
unbacked by a curated, source-linked record.
- Existing-booking hand-off and optional save-and-return: A single call-to-action hands the
traveller into Vietnam Airlines' existing search flow with origin, destination and dates pre-filled; we
do not touch search-results ranking, payment or checkout. If a traveller saves a route but does not
proceed, an optional, consent-based reminder (for example, after 24 hours) restates their saved trip
and invites them back, with no false urgency or fare-hold claims.
7. Overview Design
- The diagram below shows how a traveller’s trip brief is parsed into structured intent, ranked against a
curated knowledge base by the explainable Veya engine, presented as source-grounded destination cards
with archetype imagery, and handed off into Vietnam Airlines’ existing, unmodified search and booking
flow. An optional save-and-return loop brings the traveller back to their saved trip if they do not proceed
immediately.
- At MVP stage the parser and destination cards call an existing language-model API rather than training a
custom model from scratch, so hackathon time is spent on the curated route/destination dataset, the
explainable ranking logic, and the hand-off design - the parts that are specific to this problem.
